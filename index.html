01 Problem Statement

India is among the world's largest generators of electronic waste, and volumes keep rising with shorter device lifecycles and rapid digital adoption.

Much of it still bypasses authorised channels: end-of-life devices are stored at home, sold to informal scrap dealers or thrown out with municipal waste. This loses recoverable materials and releases toxic substances such as lead, mercury and cadmium.

Local data on how much e-waste is generated, where it accumulates and how it moves is limited. A systematic field study is therefore required to quantify e-waste flows and to plan a cost-effective collection and transportation network.

Why the system fails today

📍
Few, scattered collection points

Drop-off is inconvenient for households.

📅
Irregular pick-up

No fixed schedule, so devices pile up or leak out.

🚛
Part-empty trips

Long hauls to authorised recyclers.

02 Introduction

🔄

Reverse Logistics

The movement of goods from the point of consumption back to a point of recovery for reuse, recycling, remanufacturing or safe disposal.

💻

E-Waste

Discarded computers, mobile phones, batteries, appliances and accessories. A high-value but hazardous reverse flow: it holds recoverable metals as well as toxic parts.

⚖️

EPR Mandate

The E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 place Extended Producer Responsibility on producers, so organised collection and transport are essential.

This study carries out field investigations in Bengaluru to measure e-waste generation, map current disposal practices and collection points, and use the data to plan the collection network: collection points, consolidation hub, vehicle routes and pick-up frequency.

03 Objectives

Primary Objective

Design a cost-effective reverse logistics and collection network for e-waste, based on field data, that increases formal collection and lowers transport cost per kilogram.

Secondary Objectives

  1. Carry out field investigations and surveys on e-waste generation and disposal practices.
  2. Identify and map existing collection points, aggregators and authorised recyclers.
  3. Quantify e-waste volumes by source type and product category.
  4. Analyse the flow, distance and cost of current collection and transportation.
  5. Develop a facility location and vehicle routing plan for e-waste collection.
  6. Recommend a collection strategy aligned with the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022.

04 Methodology

1

Identification of Study Area and Stakeholders

  • Select Bengaluru zones with high e-waste generation: RVCE campus area, residential layouts, IT corridors, commercial hubs.
  • Map generators, collection points, scrap dealers, aggregators and authorised recyclers.
2

Field Investigation and Data Collection

  • Structured surveys and interviews with households, students, offices and retailers.
  • Record at each source: quantity and category of e-waste (kg/month), storage period and current disposal route (formal/informal), GPS location and distance to the nearest collection point.
  • Log collection vehicle trips: distance, time, load and cost.
3

Dataset Preparation

4

Data Analysis

  • Descriptive statistics of e-waste generation by source type and zone.
  • Estimate generation per household / institution and project total collectable volume.
  • Analyse formal vs informal disposal share and barriers to participation.
5

Logistics Planning and Model Development

  • Facility location model (p-median / set covering) for collection points and hub.
  • Vehicle routing model (CVRP) for scheduled pick-up runs to the hub.
  • Output: number and location of collection points, fleet size, routes, pick-up frequency and cost per kg.
6

Validation

  • Compare the planned routes and cost against current (as-is) practice.
  • Sensitivity analysis on volume, participation rate and fuel cost; evaluate total distance, cost per tonne and vehicle utilisation.

08 Proposed Logistics Plan

Field data from the surveys and audits will size a three-tier reverse logistics network.

Tier 1

Collection Points

Bins, kiosks, drives and retailer take-back

milk runs →
Tier 2

Consolidation Hub

Sorting, weighing, storage and data

full truck loads →
Tier 3

Authorised Recycler

Dismantling and material recovery

🚚 Scheduled milk runs

Pick-up runs are planned as scheduled milk runs, with frequency set by fill rate and distance. This cuts empty running and cost per kg while meeting the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022.

🎯 Expected outcomes

  • Higher formal collection share
  • Lower transport cost per kg
  • Better vehicle utilisation
